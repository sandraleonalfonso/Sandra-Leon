My research examines decentralisation and federalism, affective polarization, and intergovernmental relations. I adopt a comparative perspective and employ quantitative methods to study the interaction between political institutions and electoral behaviour.

## Current research lines

### Artificial intelligence and multilevel governance

I study the impact of artificial intelligence on multilevel governance and democratic accountability, as part of the project AI-GOV (PID2025-174631NB-I00), funded by the Spanish National Research Agency (AEI).

### Affective polarization and democracy

**Polarization and cooperation.** With Ignacio Jurado and Albert Falcó. Large-scale behavioral experiments in Spain, Brazil and the United States testing whether affective polarization weakens everyday cooperation. The main findings so far:

- People help less, and with lower quality, when the beneficiary backs their most disliked party.
- Out-group animosity matters more than in-group affection.
- Personal rewards raise cooperation more than public-good incentives.
- Political tasks reduce cooperation.
- Polarization spills over into minimal, impersonal cooperation.

Paper in progress: *Partisan animosity and cooperation: A Behavioral Experiment* (with I. Jurado and A. Falcó).

**Losers' consent.** I am a collaborator in [CONSENT](https://ignaciojurado.com/consent), an ERC Consolidator Grant project led by Ignacio Jurado at IPP-CSIC (grant agreement No. 101170551). CONSENT studies why electoral losers accept being governed by the winners, and when that acceptance breaks down, using surveys, experiments and qualitative methods across more than 20 democracies.

Paper in progress: *Losers' Consent: Foundations, Causes, and Consequences of an Elusive Concept* (with I. Jurado), for the *Annual Review of Political Science*.

## Past projects

### FEDCRISIS: Federalism Under Challenge: Crisis, Polarization and Populism

*El federalismo ante sus desafíos: crisis, polarización y populismo.* Funded by the Agencia Estatal de Investigación (AEI), PID2021-128287NB-I00. With Prof. Amuitz Garmendia (UC3M).

The project studies how crises, rising polarization and populist dynamics reshape multilevel governance and citizens' preferences on the territorial distribution of power. It has three strands:

1. **Crisis and centralization.** A formal model and cross-national survey experiments on how shocks such as COVID-19 shift support toward centralized or decentralized authority.
2. **Polarization and accountability.** Conceptualizing and measuring territorial affective polarization and its effects on electoral accountability.
3. **Populism and center–periphery conflict.** How radical populist parties adapt their strategies across regions, and how this shapes attitudes on the center–periphery divide.

### REACCT: Backlash on Climate Change

Co-PIs Sandra León and Lluís Orriols (UC3M and UAM), in collaboration with the European Climate Foundation. Phases: September 2022 to August 2023, and May to December 2024. Six researchers.

A longitudinal online panel (YouGov, 5,000 respondents with demographic and regional quotas; three waves in 2022–23 and two in 2024) tracking Spaniards' knowledge, attitudes and behaviour on climate policy. The project identifies six attitudinal profiles, from "backlash" to "greener", and runs survey experiments, for example on support for low-emission zones. Outputs include wave and cross-wave reports.
